How we will check that these are right
One test governs all six: ad spend per delivered order against what a delivered order earns after platform fees and product cost. Each step above should push that gap closed, week on week. If spend rises and the gap does not close, the step is not working, whatever the sales line shows. Until cost of goods arrives, we track ad spend per delivered order and delivered orders per ₹1,000 of ad spend.

Terms used in this dashboard

SKU: one product as sold, for example Throat Soothing Gummies.
Order: one customer purchase. Unit: one pack. One order can hold several units.
Revenue: sales value in our exports, before platform fees. Amazon, Instamart and website figures exclude GST; Blinkit includes it.
Price per unit: revenue divided by units. Revenue per order (AOV, average order value): revenue divided by orders.
Session: one visit to the website. Conversion rate: the share of sessions or clicks that end in an order.
Impressions: times an ad or listing was shown. Click-through rate (CTR): clicks divided by impressions. Cost per click (CPC): spend divided by clicks.
Return on ad spend (ROAS): sales credited to ads divided by ad spend. 1.0 means ads brought back what they cost, before product cost.
ACoS (Amazon): ad spend divided by sales credited to ads. TACoS (Amazon): ad spend divided by all Amazon sales. Lower is better for both.
Sales not credited to ads: Amazon sales that Amazon does not link to an ad click, often called organic sales.
Weeks of cover: how many weeks current stock lasts at the recent selling rate.
FBA / FBM: stock held in Amazon’s warehouse (Fulfilled by Amazon) or in Releaf’s own (Fulfilled by Merchant).
Placed / delivered: an order is placed when the customer pays or confirms; it counts as sales only once delivered and not returned.
Dark store: a small quick-commerce warehouse (Blinkit, Instamart) that delivers within minutes.
Listed price: the selling price on Amazon, used to value stock. It is not the cost of making the product.

Products: what sells, and how it moves over time

ChannelAll channels, with a filter for each channelPeriod10 Sep to 28 Oct for all channels; Blinkit history from 15 Mar to 16 Nov; Amazon reports to about 18 NovSourceOrder exports, Amazon product reports of 22 Oct, 13 Nov and 20 Nov, Google Ads report for 13 to 19 Nov
The questionWhich products sell, are they selling steadily or in one-off bursts, and is that the same place the revenue comes from?
Why we look at thisA business this size runs on two or three products. The rest take stock, ad spend and attention. To set targets for the next six weeks or six months, the sales team also needs to see how each product moved week by week, not only its total.
In shortThree products earn 61% of revenue: Throat Soothing, Knee Ease and Pulmo Well. Throat Soothing is the only product that sold every week, rising from 3 units in the first, 3-day week (10 to 12 Sep) to 15 in the week of 11 Oct, with no single bulk week, and Amazon’s reports show it still selling in November. Six listed products sold nothing between 10 Sep and 28 Oct. Two of them, Glyco Steady and Kids Multivitamin, were among the most advertised products on Google in 13 to 19 Nov.

Units and revenue over time

What the charts show

Pick a product, a channel and a step. The top chart shows units sold as bars, coloured by channel, and revenue as a line. The bottom chart shows the number of orders as bars and the price per unit as a line. Each channel shows only the dates we have data for: choose Blinkit to see eight months of history.

Reading them together

Revenue is units times price, so when the bars and the revenue line move together, the price held. If units rise but revenue does not, cheaper packs or products sold. Compare orders with units to spot bulk buying: a week where units jump but orders do not means a few customers bought many packs, which may not repeat. A product that sells a little every week is easier to forecast than one with one big week and long gaps.

Amazon stock: how long it lasts and where cash is sitting

ChannelAmazon stock only (Amazon’s warehouse, FBA, and Releaf’s own, FBM, as listed on Amazon). Stock at Blinkit, Instamart or offline is not included.PeriodStock on 22 Oct, 13 Nov and 20 Nov 2026. Selling rate from 10 Sep to 28 Oct, all channels.SourceAmazon product reports, order exports
The questionIs there enough Amazon stock to sell more, where is money tied up in slow stock, and is stock moving in ways sales do not explain?
Why we look at thisWeeks of cover turns a stock count into a length of time, which is what a sales team can plan against. The same number shows where cash is sitting still.
In shortStock is not what limits sales. ₹40.61L at listed price sits in 8 SKUs with more than 36 weeks of cover, and ₹32.72L of that is Hand Calm and Arthrite, which sold 6 units between 10 Sep and 28 Oct and 4 Amazon orders in about 12 to 18 Nov, after about 2,800 units were added to their Amazon stock. Only Mind Calm sells and is close to empty. More advertising will not clear these piles at the rate they are selling. Separately, between 22 Oct and 13 Nov 376 units left Amazon stock on eight products while the 13 Nov Amazon report shows only 4 orders for them, and the reports do not say where the stock went.

Units in Amazon stock on each report date, the change from 22 Oct to 20 Nov, and the Amazon orders the three product reports show for the same product. “Read” flags a product when its stock fell between 22 Oct and 13 Nov by more than about twice what its recent Amazon orders could account for, or when stock was added. Knee Ease and Arthrite also have a second Amazon listing held by Releaf (FBM), with 1,312 and 1,375 units on 20 Nov; these are not added here because they may be the same stock listed twice.

Reading it together

Stock should fall roughly in step with orders. Between 22 Oct and 13 Nov it fell much faster than that on several products with few or no orders: Kids Multivitamin went from 95 to 1, RecharZ from 40 to 1, Mind Calm from 31 to 1, Sharp from 50 to 12, Cradle Bliss Women from 40 to 5, Smooth Vac from 37 to 15, Pulmo Well from 38 to 1 and Sleep Help from 182 to 101. That points to stock being removed or moved, for example to another channel. In the same period Hand Calm rose by about 1,480 units, Arthrite by about 1,330, and Pulmo Well was restocked. The reports do not say why, so we have asked the team.

06Channel costs

Channel costs: Blinkit, Instamart, Amazon ads and Google ads

ChannelBlinkit and Instamart (quick commerce), Amazon ads, Google ads for the websitePeriodBlinkit 15 Mar to 16 Nov; Amazon campaigns as exported on 20 Nov; Google 13 to 19 Nov; Instamart as stated on the callsSourceBlinkit sales and payout reports, Amazon campaign list, Google Ads report, weekly calls
The questionAfter the platform takes its cut, which channels make money and which consume it?
Why we look at thisRevenue is what a channel shows. Net payout is what it hands over. The gap is commission, shipping and storage, and on a quick-commerce platform that gap can be bigger than the revenue itself.
In shortReleaf’s team said on the 6 Nov call that Blinkit is not working. The payouts show why: storage, not commission, took more than the channel sold, so Releaf owes ₹18,755. Instamart, on the figures given on the call, has paid out about two thirds of its sales. On Amazon, nearly half the campaign spend in the latest snapshot bought no sale, while the two campaigns that defend the brand name paid back 2.4 and 4.8 times.

07Amazon ads

Amazon ads: why ad spend is not turning into sales

ChannelAmazon onlyPeriod15 Sep to 14 Oct, 5 to 12 Nov and 13 to 20 Nov 2026SourceAmazon account reports, Amazon product report of 20 Nov, weekly calls
The questionOn Amazon, what is holding sales back: people not seeing the listings, not clicking, not buying, products out of stock, or ads that cost too much?
Why we look at thisA marketplace sells through four things: being seen, being chosen, having stock and paying a sensible amount for ads. Each fails in a way the others cannot repair, so the fix depends on which one broke.
In shortAmazon is being seen but not chosen. In 13 to 20 Nov the listings were shown 6.52 lakh times, opened 1,354 times and converted 1.55% of the time, with no rating on any listing. The higher-bid period since about 4 Nov bought sales at ₹3 of ad spend for every ₹1 of ad sales. It also coincides with more sales not credited to ads, which is the sign the higher bids are meant to produce. It needs a date by which TACoS must fall.

what the direction of each means
What the grid shows

ACoS is ad spend against sales credited to ads. TACoS is ad spend against all Amazon sales. The grid shows what each combination of the two moving up or down usually means.

Reading the two together

If ACoS rises while TACoS falls, ads are lifting organic sales and will pay back later. If both rise, as here, total sales are not growing as fast as spend. That is expected in the first weeks of higher bids and during Diwali bidding. It becomes a problem if TACoS is still rising two to three weeks after the higher bids started.
